The most expensive fifteen minutes in a factory

Aditya Swaroop, for Powertown's Machine Learning Engineer, Intern (Term-Time) application. October 2026.

A Powerblock earns its keep in one 15-minute interval a month, plus a few dozen summer afternoons when the grid calls. I built an open instrument around that fact. It finds the Massachusetts buildings where a 250 kW / 522 kWh battery pays, prices each one under the real 2026 National Grid and Eversource tariffs, and runs the battery without seeing the future. Every result is reproducible and audited.

https://as567-code.github.io/fifteen/Interactive page. Value your own Green Button data in the browser, replay any site with any controller, and read the audit trail.

Four findings

78% of the ceilingA forecast-driven controller kept this share of the perfect-foresight value on 154 real metered customers in a year no model had seen. A tuned rule-of-thumb kept 58%; a fixed daily window kept 8%.
1.2 vs 0.4 months off the billMedian library building under Eversource G-2 Boston vs National Grid G-3 ($41,587 vs $12,217 a year). National Grid bills transmission per kWh, which shaving cannot touch, so territory matters more than building type.
$44,899 vs $14,133Average National Grid building with ConnectedSolutions Daily Dispatch stacked on demand savings, vs demand savings alone. Co-optimizing the two gives up only 13% of demand savings (5% under Eversource).
Load factor predicts almost nothingAcross 958 sites, load factor barely ranks a Powerblock's value. The energy in the top 250 kW of the hardest day's load does (rank correlation -0.84). A bill cannot show it; 15-minute data can. A twelve-bill screener still triages prospects with 14% median error on metered sites.

Controller performance, 2014 test year (Eversource G-2 billing rule)

Share of the oracle's billed-kW reduction kept, summed over 1,848 site-months. Model trained on 2012, every controller tuned on 2013 (baselines too), 2014 run once. The forecast controller never billed a month above the no-battery bill.

How it works

  • Exact perfect-foresight dispatch. A greedy dispatch rule plus bisection finds the lowest achievable billed demand each month. It handles National Grid's peak window and Eversource's 55% off-peak discount, and it is fast enough to run in a browser. An independent HiGHS linear program confirms it to within 0.05 kW.
  • Probabilistic forecasting. One gradient-boosted quantile model is trained across sites on scale-free load, so a new site with four weeks of data has a forecast on day one. Its 95% band covered 94.5% of actual 2014 intervals.
  • Sunk-peak control. The controller re-plans every 15 minutes against today's forecast, corrected by the meter. It never defends below the month's already-set peak, since shaving below it is worth nothing and drains energy the hardest day will need, and it never recharges above it, so it cannot create a peak of its own.
  • Value stacking. A month-by-month LP co-optimizes demand charges with $200/kW ConnectedSolutions Daily Dispatch. Events go on ISO-NE's 42 highest-load summer days of 2018.
